# Figure captions (self-sufficient)

Each caption states what is plotted, for which site and scenario, in which
units, and which results file it is drawn from. Figures are regenerated by
`python src/pipeline.py figures` into `results/figures/` (300 dpi PNG + PDF).
Colours are colour-blind-safe; every series is also identified by marker,
line style and a direct label, so the figures read correctly in greyscale.

**Figure 1. Model structure.** Household demand (RAMP appliance loads from
MTF ownership rates plus discrete cooking events, with shiftable fraction φ)
feeds an hourly photovoltaic–battery dispatch over a 20-year project life;
a least-cost search sizes PV, storage and a load-sized inverter subject to
unmet demand of at most 5% in every project year; a financial model returns
LCOE, NPV and IRR and three inverse solvers (capital grant, cost-reflective
tariff, operating subsidy). Dashed arrows are exogenous inputs.

**Figure 2. Modelled household cooking load by hour of day.** Mean electric
cooking load per cooking household (W), 300 households, 1 kW electric
pressure cooker, ten random seeds, φ = 0. Shaded bands are the mealtime
windows observed in MECS metered stove data (Scott et al., 2025). Timing
spread calibrated to the Kenyan diversity factor (9.75); peak hour, three-peak
shape and night minimum are validated features. Source:
`results/cooking_validation_summary.csv`.

**Figure 3. Levelised cost and least-cost storage against eCooking
penetration, flat tariff.** (a) LCOE (US$/kWh, real, 12% discount rate);
(b) least-cost battery capacity (kWh) meeting ≤5% unmet demand in every
project year. 300-household archetypes at Nkombo (Rwanda) and Ringiti
(Kenya), representative-year NASA POWER resource. Source:
`results/scenarios_both.csv`.

**Figure 4. Avoided battery capacity against the daytime (09:00–16:00)
discount, full eCooking penetration.** Difference in least-cost storage
(kWh) between matched flat and time-of-use tariff arms, by the share φ of
evening cooking households shift into the afternoon. The modelled response is
a step: any positive discount triggers the full φ, so avoided storage does
not scale with the size of the discount. Source: `results/tou_sweep.csv`.

**Figure 5. Blended residential tariff by monthly household consumption.**
US$/kWh, Rwanda (RURA, effective 1 October 2025, telescopic) and Kenya
(EPRA 2023, non-telescopic), converted at RWF 1,460 and KES 129 per US$.
Markers show the simulated mean household with full eCooking in project
years 1 and 20 (5%/yr demand growth). Source:
`results/tariff_band_recomputed.csv`.

**Figure 6. Flat tariff required for viability against the real discount
rate.** Cost-reflective tariff (US$/kWh, logarithmic scale) at which the
project IRR equals the discount rate plus three percentage points, full
eCooking penetration, flat tariff, no capital grant; dotted lines are the
regulated residential rates. Source: `results/discount_sweep.csv`.
